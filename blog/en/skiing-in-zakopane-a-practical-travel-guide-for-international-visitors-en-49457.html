Skiing in Zakopane: A Practical Travel Guide for International Visitors

Zakopane, Poland's winter capital nestled at the foot of the Tatra Mountains, has become a favourite destination for skiers travelling from across Europe and beyond. If you're flying in for a ski trip, getting from Katowice-Pyrzowice Airport to the slopes smoothly is just as important as choosing the right resort. Here's a practical guide to help you plan your journey.

Why Fly into Katowice-Pyrzowice?

Katowice-Pyrzowice Airport (KTW) is one of the most convenient gateways for reaching both Zakopane and Kraków, serving numerous low-cost and international carriers. While it's not the closest airport to the Tatras, it offers frequent connections and competitive flight prices, making it a popular entry point for skiers heading south. The journey from the airport to Zakopane takes approximately 2.5 to 3 hours by road, depending on traffic and weather conditions.

Planning Your Transfer in Advance

Winter travel in southern Poland can be unpredictable, with snow and icy roads common from December through March. Booking a private transfer in advance is strongly recommended over relying on public transport or last-minute taxis, especially if you're arriving late at night or in a large group with ski equipment. A pre-arranged transfer means your driver will track your flight, wait for delays, and take you directly to your accommodation in Zakopane without the stress of navigating unfamiliar roads in winter conditions.

Choosing the Right Vehicle

Ski trips come with extra luggage—boots, skis, snowboards, and bulky winter clothing—so selecting the right vehicle matters. For couples or small families, a Chrysler Pacifica or Mercedes Vito offers comfortable seating with ample space for ski bags and suitcases. Larger groups or families travelling together often prefer the Mercedes Sprinter, which comfortably accommodates passengers and equipment without compromising on comfort during the drive through mountain roads. For bigger groups, such as ski clubs or corporate trips, a coach transfer ensures everyone travels together, with enough room for all gear.

What to Pack and Prepare

Before your flight, make sure your ski equipment is properly packed in protective bags, as most airlines charge extra for oversized luggage. Check your airline's specific policies regarding ski and snowboard carriage, as allowances vary. It's also wise to carry a change of warm clothing in your hand luggage in case checked baggage is delayed—Zakopane's climate can be significantly colder than your departure city.

Timing Your Arrival

If possible, try to arrive in Zakopane with daylight remaining, particularly if it's your first visit. This allows you to settle into your accommodation, pick up any rental equipment, and get a feel for the town before hitting the slopes the next day. Mountain roads can be more difficult to navigate after dark, especially in snowy conditions, so an early afternoon arrival is ideal when scheduling your flight and transfer.

Combining Zakopane with Kraków

Many international visitors choose to combine their ski trip with a stop in Kraków, either before or after their time in the mountains. Since transfers between Katowice-Pyrzowice, Kraków, and Zakopane run along similar routes, it's easy to arrange a multi-stop itinerary with one transfer provider, saving time and simplifying logistics.
