Kraków for Foreign Tourists — First Steps After Landing

2026-09-25

Landing at Katowice-Pyrzowice Airport is often the first real moment of your Polish adventure, especially if Kraków is your final destination. While the airport itself is compact and easy to navigate, knowing what to do in the first hour after touchdown can save you stress, time, and unnecessary expense. Here's a practical guide to help you get started on the right foot.

Clear the Airport Quickly

Katowice-Pyrzowice (KTW) is a small regional airport, so immigration and baggage claim are usually fast, even during peak season. Follow the signs for "Exit" or "Arrivals" and head toward the main terminal hall. ATMs and currency exchange kiosks are available near the arrivals area, but exchange rates here can be less favorable than in the city center — withdrawing a small amount of Polish złoty (PLN) for immediate needs is usually enough.

Getting to Kraków: Know Your Options

Katowice-Pyrzowice is roughly 80-90 minutes from Kraków by road, and there is no direct train connection, which surprises many first-time visitors. Public transport options involve a combination of shuttle buses to Katowice city center and then a train or bus to Kraków — convenient for backpackers, but time-consuming with luggage.

For travelers who value comfort and predictability, a private transfer is the simplest solution. Booking in advance with a company offering airport pickups means your driver will be waiting in the arrivals hall with a name sign, ready to help with luggage and get you on the road immediately — no queues, no navigating unfamiliar bus schedules in a foreign language.

Depending on your group size, vehicles range from a comfortable Mercedes Vito for small groups or families, to a Mercedes Sprinter or bus for larger parties traveling together. A Chrysler Pacifica is a popular choice for couples or solo travelers wanting extra comfort and space after a long flight.

What to Expect on the Road

The drive from Pyrzowice to Kraków is mostly highway, passing through Silesian industrial landscapes before opening up into the greener Lesser Poland region. It's a good time to rest, check into your accommodation online, or ask your driver for local tips — many transfer drivers are happy to share recommendations for restaurants or attractions.

Arriving in Kraków: First Impressions

Most visitors are dropped off directly at their hotel or apartment, ideally close to the Old Town or Kazimierz district, both very walkable and packed with cafés, historic sites, and nightlife. Once settled, take a slow first walk toward the Main Market Square (Rynek Główny) — it's the best way to get oriented and adjust after travel.

Practical Tips for the First Day

Keep your accommodation address written down or saved offline, as Wi-Fi isn't guaranteed everywhere immediately. Carry some cash in PLN, since not every small café or market stall accepts cards. Public transport within Kraków (trams and buses) is efficient and easy once you get your bearings, but for the airport-to-city journey itself, pre-arranged transport remains the most stress-free choice, especially after a long international flight.

Whether you're heading straight into Kraków's Old Town or continuing onward to Zakopane for the mountains, starting your trip with a smooth, reliable transfer sets the tone for the rest of your stay in Poland.
