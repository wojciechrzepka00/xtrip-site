Kraków for Foreign Tourists — First Steps After Landing

Landing at Katowice-Pyrzowice Airport is often the first real moment of a Polish adventure, but for many travellers heading to Kraków, the excitement is mixed with a simple question: what now? The airport itself is compact and easy to navigate, yet the journey onward requires a little planning. Here's a practical guide to help you get your bearings and start your trip smoothly.

Clearing the Airport

Katowice-Pyrzowice (airport code KTW) is a mid-sized regional airport, so immigration and baggage claim rarely take long. Free Wi-Fi is available throughout the terminal, which is useful for checking messages from your driver or confirming pickup details before you step outside. There are currency exchange counters and ATMs near the arrivals hall, though rates there are not always the most favourable — if possible, withdraw only a small amount for immediate needs and exchange the rest later in Kraków's Old Town, where competitive kantor (exchange office) rates are common.

Getting From the Airport to Kraków

There is no train line connecting Pyrzowice directly to Kraków, which surprises some visitors. Public transport options involve a bus to Katowice followed by a train, a combination that can easily take two to three hours with transfers and waiting time. For travellers arriving after a long flight, this is rarely the most comfortable choice. A pre-booked private transfer remains the simplest solution: a driver waits for you in the arrivals hall, helps with luggage, and takes you directly to your hotel or apartment in Kraków, typically within 75–90 minutes depending on traffic. For solo travellers or couples, a Mercedes Vito or Chrysler Pacifica offers a quick, private ride, while families or small groups often prefer the extra space of a Mercedes Sprinter. Larger groups, such as tour parties or wedding guests, can be accommodated with a coach transfer.

First Impressions of Kraków

Once you arrive in Kraków, the Old Town (Stare Miasto) is the natural starting point. Most hotels are located within or close to this historic core, making it easy to explore on foot. The Main Market Square (Rynek Główny), St. Mary's Basilica, and the Cloth Hall are all within a short walk of one another, and the entire district is compact enough to get oriented within your first afternoon.

If your flight lands in the morning, resist the urge to collapse into bed immediately — a short walk around the Old Town or a visit to Wawel Castle can help you adjust to the new time zone faster. For evening arrivals, it's often best to check in, have a simple dinner nearby, and save sightseeing for the next day.

Practical Tips for the First Day

Keep a printed or downloaded copy of your hotel address and booking confirmation, as not every taxi driver or local speaks fluent English, and having addresses in Polish can save time. Public transport within Kraków (trams and buses) is efficient and affordable, with ticket machines available on board accepting cards. Finally, if you're planning a day trip to Zakopane or the Tatra Mountains later in your stay, consider arranging transport in advance — the route takes around two hours and is far more comfortable in a private vehicle than on a crowded minibus.

With a little preparation, the trip from Pyrzowice to Kraków can be the easiest part of your journey — leaving you with more energy to enjoy the city itself.
