Weather in Silesia and Krakow: What to Wear for Your Airport Transfer

2026-11-05

Landing at Katowice-Pyrzowice Airport and heading to Krakow or Zakopano means crossing different micro-climates in a short time. Silesia tends to be flatter and slightly milder, while Krakow sits in a river valley that traps humidity, and Zakopane, close to the Tatra Mountains, can feel like a different season altogether. Knowing what to pack and wear makes the transfer, and the days that follow, much more comfortable.

Spring (March to May)

Spring in this region is unpredictable. Mornings can be cold enough for a jacket, while afternoons warm up quickly. Rain showers are common, especially in April.

  • Bring a waterproof or windproof jacket you can easily remove.
  • Layering works best: a t-shirt, a light sweater, and a shell jacket.
  • Comfortable closed shoes are a good idea, as pavements can be wet at the airport and in city centers.

If you are continuing straight to Zakopane, keep in mind that mountain weather changes faster than in Krakow or Katowice, so pack an extra warm layer for the evening.

Summer (June to August)

Summers can be warm and occasionally humid, particularly in Krakow's old town area. Evenings cool down, especially if your final stop is in the mountains.

  • Light, breathable clothing is fine for daytime travel.
  • Carry a thin layer or light scarf for air-conditioned vehicles, since our fleet, including the Mercedes Vito and Mercedes Sprinter, runs climate control during the ride.
  • Sudden summer storms do happen, so a compact umbrella or rain jacket in your carry-on is a practical choice.
  • If Zakopane is your destination, pack a warmer top even in July or August, as mountain evenings can be noticeably cooler than in the lowlands.

Autumn (September to November)

Autumn starts mild but turns cold and damp by late October. Fog is common in the mornings, both around the airport and along the route to Krakow.

  • A medium-weight jacket and a scarf will cover most September and October days.
  • By November, think in terms of winter layers: hat, gloves, and a warmer coat.
  • Waterproof shoes are worth it, as rain and wet leaves make pavements slippery.

Winter (December to February)

Winters in Silesia and Krakow are cold, with temperatures often below freezing and occasional snowfall. Zakopane and the surrounding Tatra foothills usually get more snow and colder temperatures than Krakow or Katowice.

  • A proper winter coat, hat, gloves, and a scarf are essential, not optional.
  • Waterproof, insulated boots with good grip help a lot when moving between the terminal, the parking area, and the vehicle.
  • If you are traveling with children or elderly family members, pack a blanket or extra layer for the ride, even though our vehicles, from the Chrysler Pacifica to the Sprinter vans and buses, are heated.

Practical Tips for the Transfer Itself

Whatever the season, keep your outerwear accessible in your hand luggage rather than packed deep in a suitcase. Our driver waits in the arrivals hall with a name sign, so you will not be standing outside in the weather while searching for transport.

Group transfers by bus or Sprinter can mean a short wait for other passengers, so having a layer ready for the Hall temperature is useful in winter and summer alike.

For the most current information on the airport itself, including any terminal changes or seasonal updates, we recommend checking the official Katowice Airport website before your trip.

Final Thoughts

Dressing in layers is the simplest strategy for a transfer across Silesia, Krakow, and the Tatra region, since conditions can shift from mild to mountain-cold within the same journey. With the right clothing and a reliable, pre-booked transfer, the trip from Katowice-Pyrzowice Airport to your final destination stays comfortable no matter the season.
